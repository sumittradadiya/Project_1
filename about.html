About Ember Lane

Coffee, taken seriously. Everything else, taken lightly.

We're a roastery first and a cafe second — the espresso machine just happens to sit ten feet from the roaster.

How it started

Two engineers, one bad cup of coffee

In 2015, Meera and Rohan were working long hours at a firm across town, surviving on instant coffee from the office pantry. A trip to a coffee farm in Chikmagalur changed how they thought about the drink entirely — they came back asking why nothing in Surat tasted like that cup.

A year of weekend cupping sessions later, they bought a 5kg drum roaster, set it up in a rented garage, and started selling filter coffee at the Sunday market on Athwa Lines. Ember Lane opened as a permanent shop on Kadamba Lane in 2016.

How we roast

Small batches, tasted daily

Every lot is roasted in batches of 12kg or less, on a machine we can see and hear from the counter. We cup each roast the next morning before it goes on the shelf — if it isn't right, it doesn't go out.

We buy the majority of our green coffee directly from three farms: a washed Arabica lot from Chikmagalur, a natural-process lot from Coorg, and a rotating single estate we bring in for a few months each year.

What we believe

A short list of things we won't compromise on

Coffee is served within 90 seconds of grinding, or not at all. Milk is steamed to order, never held. Pastries that don't sell out by evening go to a shelter kitchen two streets away, not into a display case the next day. And nobody on staff is asked to upsell a drink someone didn't ask for.
